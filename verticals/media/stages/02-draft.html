A genuinely different reasoning shape

IOF Core, GoodRelations, FIBO, and FHIR are all fundamentally descriptive — they model what exists and how things relate. ODRL is deontic: it models what's permitted, prohibited, or owed, under what conditions. A Permission and a Prohibition can target the very same asset and action — the ontology's job is to represent that both exist, not to resolve which one wins (real-world rights conflicts often need a human or a court to actually decide that).

Classes adopted from ODRL

ClassDefinition (from the spec)
PolicyThe umbrella concept, with three real subtypes: Set (generic rules), Offer ("offerings of Rules from assigner Parties"), Agreement ("granting of Rules from assigner to assignee Parties"). The Offer/Agreement distinction directly resolves the "License" glossary collision.
Permission"The ability to exercise an Action over an Asset."
Prohibition"The inability to exercise an Action over an Asset."
Duty"The obligation to exercise an agreed Action" — covers the attribution and royalty requirements directly.
Asset"A resource... that are the subject of a Rule" — covers both a digital photo and, for the real-estate half of this vertical, a physical studio space.

A real semantic distinction worth using precisely

ODRL's own action definitions distinguish sell/give (ownership transfer, "while deleting the original asset") from use/license (a right granted without deleting or transferring the original). This maps directly onto POL-9010's Sales-vs-signed-terms conflict — not a modeling choice invented for this vertical, but a distinction ODRL's spec already makes explicit.

Where ODRL doesn't reach

ODRL models rights and obligations exhaustively, but has no notion of "an asset registry export," "a facilities booking calendar," or "a royalty reconciliation process." Those need domain-specific vocabulary, checked against ODRL first.

One deliberate non-invention: ODRL's real action vocabulary has no dedicated "lease" or "rent" action. Rather than invent one for the studio-space conflict, Resolve models studio booking as odrl:use constrained by spatial/timeInterval operands — both real ODRL constraint left-operands — which is a more precise fit than a fabricated action would have been.
