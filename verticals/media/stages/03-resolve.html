Conflict 1 of 10
Parent decision — resolve first

Does "License" mean any agreed deal, or a fully executed Agreement?

Sales uses it for anything agreed to, even pending signature. Rights/Legal reserves it for countersigned terms.

Recommended — ODRL, used precisely
Model Sales' meaning as odrl:Offer and Legal's as odrl:Agreement — real, distinct ODRL subtypes of Policy. A deal only becomes an Agreement at countersignature; before that, it's an Offer no matter what Sales calls it internally.
Conflict 2 of 10

Asset ID, syndication ID, and creator catalog number don't all exist for every asset

AMB-40013 has no syndication ID. AMB-40014 has no creator catalog number.

Recommended — ODRL + domain
Keep all three as independent identifiers on Asset — ODRL doesn't require any particular identifier scheme. Leave genuinely missing ones absent rather than inferred, same discipline as every prior vertical's missing-identifier conflict.
Conflict 3 of 10 — highest stakes

A Permission was granted without checking a still-active Prohibition on the same asset

POL-9001 (Agreement, distribute, Northwind Publishing) doesn't reference POL-9002 (Prohibition, predates it, forbids distribution to competing outdoor apparel brands).

Recommended — ODRL, used as intended
Assert both Permission and Prohibition on the same Asset — ODRL explicitly allows this, because real-world rights conflicts genuinely can require a human or legal review to resolve, not an automatic rule. Flag the pair with a domain conflictsWithProhibition: true so it surfaces for review rather than silently coexisting unnoticed, which is what actually happened here.
Conflict 4 of 10

POL-9010 was called "sold" internally, but its signed terms say otherwise

ODRL's own sell/give actions mean ownership transfer "while deleting the original asset." license grants use without deleting or transferring anything.

Recommended — ODRL, used precisely
Assert the real action license on POL-9010, matching the signed terms — not sell. Flag the record with a domain internalLabelDisagreesWithTerms: true so Sales' casual "sold" language doesn't quietly become the client's (or Amberlane's own future staff's) understanding of what was actually granted.
Conflict 5 of 10

POL-9001's license window differs between two systems

Rights-management: ends 2025-12-31. Billing: no end date, still "current."

Recommended — ODRL, with a different shape than prior verticals
Unlike the Prohibition/Permission conflict, this isn't two legitimately independent facts — rights-management is definitionally the system of record for a Policy's timeInterval. Model billing's copy as derived from it with its own lastSynced date, and flag it stale rather than treating both figures as equally authoritative. Not every two-system disagreement is a "keep both" case; some have a real canonical source.
Conflict 6 of 10

Harbor Sunrise was used outside its licensed territory

POL-9001's spatial constraint: North America. 2025 audit found usage in France and Germany.

Recommended — domain-specific
Record the actual usage as a domain UsageEvent linked to the Agreement, flagged withinSpatialConstraint: false — makes the violation a permanent, queryable fact rather than something that only ever lived in a one-off audit report.
Conflict 7 of 10 — distinct from Conflict 6

Nothing routinely checks usage against spatial constraints

REQ-402 is satisfied by the spatial field simply existing — not by anything that verifies usage against it. Conflict 6 is the one violation an audit happened to catch; this is the missing general mechanism.

Recommended — domain-specific
Model a recurring domain ComplianceCheck tied to each Agreement's constraints, and flag its current absence explicitly with hasScheduledComplianceCheck: false — the gap is structural, not specific to POL-9001.
Conflict 8 of 10

An attribution Duty's fulfillment was never verified

REQ-401 is satisfied by the Duty clause existing on the Agreement — not by the credit actually appearing in the published work.

Recommended — ODRL + domain
Keep odrl:Duty (with action attribute) as the obligation itself — that part is real and correct. Add a domain dutyFulfillmentVerified: false flag, since ODRL models the obligation but not runtime fulfillment-tracking. Same checkpoint-gap pattern as every prior vertical.
Conflict 9 of 10 — the real-estate half

Studio A is booked twice for the same window

Facilities calendar: internal photography, 09:00–17:00. Rights-management: Vantage Ad Group shoot (POL-9020), 08:00–14:00. Neither system knows about the other's booking.

Recommended — ODRL, no invented "lease" action
Model both bookings as odrl:use Permissions on the same physical Asset (Studio A), each with its own timeInterval constraint — the overlap becomes directly visible as two Permissions with conflicting temporal scope on one asset, structurally identical to Conflict 3's Permission/Prohibition clash, just Permission-vs-Permission this time. No dedicated "lease" action was invented — ODRL's real vocabulary doesn't have one, and use plus real constraint operands covers this precisely.
Conflict 10 of 10

Finance and Rights/Legal disagree on Jamie Tran's royalty percentage

Finance's payment system: 12%. Rights/Legal's countersigned contract file: 15%. Never reconciled.

Recommended — ODRL
The countersigned contract file is the canonical source for a Duty's percentage term — same "one system is definitionally authoritative" reasoning as Conflict 5. Model Finance's running figure as a separate PaymentRecord, flagged when it diverges from the contracted percentage, rather than silently trusting whichever number Finance happened to enter.
All ten resolved. Next: Stage 4 — Validate.
