Stage 3 — Resolve

One conflict at a time, in dependency order. Reveal the recommended answer, then move on — same discipline the method itself requires: "never batch conflicts." Vocabulary below comes from real ontologies (schema.org, W3C SOSA/SSN, W3C PROV-O, W3C SKOS) plus real external standards represented via SKOS (SAE J3016, ISO 26262) — see Stage 2 for where each came from.

Conflict 1 of 10
Parent decision — resolve first

Is "automation level" a fixed property of the Ridgeline R4 platform, or does it depend on where and when a trip happens?

The fleet registry lists "L4" as marketed level on every row, uniformly. Actual operational level varies by metro and permit: true driverless in Dunmore, supervised in Millbrook, and inconsistent even within Millbrook (FLT-0244).

Recommended — schema.org + SKOS
Keep marketedAutomationLevel as a static schema:Vehicle property, referencing a fixed skos:Concept from the SAE J3016 scheme. Model operationalAutomationLevel as a separate, per-trip claim tied to metro/permit context, not a second static property on the vehicle. This is the parent decision because Conflicts 8 and 9 both depend on "operational level" being trip-scoped, not vehicle-scoped.
Conflict 2 of 10

FLT-0231 has denoted two physically different vehicles

Fleet ID FLT-0231 was assigned to VIN ...100111 until it was written off in INC-0442, then reassigned to replacement VIN ...100558 six weeks later. The telematics dashboard still labels pre-swap trip logs under plain "FLT-0231," with no VIN qualifier.

Recommended — schema.org + domain denotes
vehicleIdentificationNumber is the ontology's canonical identity anchor — one schema:Vehicle instance per VIN. FLT-0231 becomes an Identifier that denotes a specific VIN at a given time, not a standing alias for "whichever vehicle currently holds this fleet ID." Any trip log has to resolve to the VIN active on the trip's date, not to today's FLT-0231 assignment — the same discipline manufacturing used for ROT-320's Rev B/Rev C identifier reuse.
Conflict 3 of 10

Do Mapping's ODD polygon, Regulatory's permit boundary, and Marketing's service-area map describe the same "supported area"?

All three claim to describe where Overland vehicles operate in Dunmore. Marketing's 15-mile circle includes the Colby Bridge corridor and Ashcombe Commons — both outside Mapping's validated ODD and outside Regulatory's permit boundary.

Recommended — SKOS + domain classes
Three distinct classes, not one: domain-specific OperationalDesignDomain (Mapping, ASAM OpenODD-informed condition scoping), domain-specific PermitBoundary (Regulatory), and domain-specific MarketingServiceArea (Product). Where Marketing's area genuinely matches the other two, use skos:closeMatch. For Ashcombe Commons and the Colby Bridge corridor, no match property at all — same discipline as manufacturing's Value Bolt case: an area that's outside both the validated ODD and the legal permit boundary isn't "approximately supported," it's unsupported, and SKOS shouldn't imply otherwise.
Conflict 4 of 10

INC-0442 has three disagreeing cause determinations, but only one is filed with NHTSA

Telemetry: planner following-distance fault. Safety driver: obstructed view, believed necessary. Engineering review: perception object-tracking loss on adjacent-lane debris was a contributing factor — this is the only one submitted under NHTSA SGO 2021-01.

Recommended — PROV-O
Model each determination as a separate prov:Attribution from a distinct prov:Agent (telemetry system, safety driver, engineering review board) on the same sosa:Observation — don't collapse to one "cause" field. Mark the engineering review's attribution with an explicit regulatoryFilingOf flag so the ontology can represent both "what actually happened, per three sources" and "what was legally reported," without conflating the two.
Depends on Conflict 4's pattern — Conflict 5 of 10

Are "Build 4.12.2" (Fleet Ops) and "perception-v7.3.1 + planning-v2.9.0 + control-v1.4.4" (Engineering) the same thing?

The 2025-06-05 repackage changed no component — only the Fleet Ops build number. Nothing in either system links the two labels to each other.

Recommended — SKOS + domain SoftwareRelease
One SoftwareRelease resource with two Identifiers — skos:exactMatch, not closeMatch, since they denote the literally identical deployed artifact. Unlike Conflict 3's geofence case, this isn't a genuine scope difference — it's purely two teams' independent naming schemes for one thing, so the highest-confidence SKOS predicate is the correct one.
Depends on Conflict 5 — Conflict 6 of 10

perception-v7.3.1 reduced pedestrian detection range below REQ-AV-014's minimum, and nothing flagged it

The release-notes template only has checkboxes for New feature / Bug fix / Performance — no field for "changed a detection-envelope value" — so the 45m→38m reduction was published as "Performance" and REQ-AV-014 (40m minimum) was never re-verified.

Recommended — domain requirementSatisfiedBy, re-verify-on-change
Any SoftwareRelease that changes a sosa:hasResult value a SafetyRequirement's requirementSatisfiedBy link depends on must flag that requirement requiresReverification automatically — not rely on a release-notes checkbox category that was never designed to catch this class of change. Same discipline as manufacturing's Conflict 8 (stale requirement links), applied here to a case with materially higher stakes: a pedestrian-detection requirement is currently unmet by production software.
Conflict 7 of 10

REQ-AV-022's ASIL rating disagrees with the current supplier component spec

Original hazard analysis HA-2023-08 rates the emergency braking subsystem ASIL-D. The current supplier spec, after a Q2 2025 redesign that removed a redundant sensor, rates the same subsystem ASIL-C. No formal re-rating review is on record.

Recommended — SKOS + PROV-O
Model ASILRating as a temporally-qualified skos:Concept assertion (from the ISO 26262 concept scheme) attributed via prov:wasAttributedTo to a specific hazard analysis document version — not a static property of the subsystem. Keep both HA-2023-08's ASIL-D and the supplier spec's ASIL-C as distinct, dated claims. Do not silently prefer either the higher or lower rating: the disagreement itself is what should block deployment sign-off pending a real re-rating review.
Depends on Conflict 3 — Conflict 8 of 10

A completed trip's origin and destination were inside the permit boundary — but was the whole route?

A pickup at Ashcombe Commons requires crossing either Colby Bridge or a 400m stretch of unincorporated Ashcombe County road — both outside Regulatory's permit boundary — even when the trip's endpoints are otherwise unremarkable.

Recommended — domain PermitBoundaryExcursion
Compliance has to be checked against the full route geometry, not just origin/destination — endpoint-only checking is exactly how Ashcombe Commons ended up as a live pickup point in the rider app despite being outside the permit boundary Conflict 3 established. Log any route segment outside PermitBoundary as a PermitBoundaryExcursion event, linked to the specific trip and the specific boundary it crossed, rather than treating the trip as compliant by default.
Depends on Conflict 1 — Conflict 9 of 10

Does the fleet-wide "safety driver required" roster field tell you whether a driver was actually present?

The roster defaults to YES for every vehicle and was never flipped to NO for Dunmore after its Deployment Permit was granted — so it can't confirm driverless trips actually happened there. In Millbrook, FLT-0244's roster says "required and scheduled," but seat-occupancy telemetry shows the seat empty on 12 of 20 recent trips — the same field failing in the opposite direction.

Recommended — SOSA
Split into two distinct properties: safetyDriverRequired (a policy rule, derived from the vehicle's permit type — schema-level, per Conflict 1's operational-level context) and safetyDriverPresent (a sosa:Observation from the seat-occupancy sensor, per trip). The roster field currently conflates a policy default with an observed fact; only the sensor observation can actually answer the Stage 0 gap question about what happened on a given trip.
Conflict 10 of 10

Mapping and Perception Engineering maintain two different max-speed figures for the same road class

Mapping's ODD register lists 45 mph as the validated max speed for arterial roads. Perception Engineering's own detection-envelope spec — based on sensor range at speed — puts reliable pedestrian detection at a maximum of 35 mph on the same road class. Neither team maintains the other's number.

Recommended — SOSA
Model each figure as a separate sosa:Observation's hasResult, attributed to its own prov:Agent (Mapping vs. Perception Engineering) via wasAssociatedWith — don't average the two speeds or treat one as authoritative by default. The 10 mph gap is itself the operationally significant fact: it means the ODD register currently permits a speed the perception stack can't reliably detect pedestrians at, which is exactly the kind of disagreement that should surface, not disappear into a single reconciled number.
