Autos / Autonomous Vehicles

Stage 2 — Draft

No single published ontology covers "autonomous vehicle fleet operations" end to end. This draft combines schema.org's Vehicle vocabulary for the vehicle itself, W3C/OGC SOSA/SSN for sensing and events, W3C PROV-O for revision and attribution, and W3C SKOS both for cross-reference confidence and for representing two real external controlled vocabularies — SAE J3016's automation levels and ISO 26262's ASIL scale — as first-class, queryable concept schemes rather than free-text strings.

0. Scope 1. Discover 2. Draft 3. Resolve 4. Validate

The pattern this draft uses: identity vs. claim vs. context

Discover surfaced the same shape three separate times: a vehicle's automation level, a subsystem's ASIL rating, and a road segment's validated speed are all treated by source documents as fixed properties, when each is actually a claim made by a specific team, at a specific time, under specific conditions. schema.org's Vehicle gives a stable identity anchor (vehicleIdentificationNumber); PROV-O and SKOS give the vocabulary for attaching time- and agent-scoped claims to that anchor instead of overwriting a single field every time someone's understanding changes.

Where real ontologies don't reach — flagged, not forced

Nothing in schema.org, SOSA/SSN, PROV-O, or SKOS models a regulatory permit or its boundary — that's a legal/administrative concept, not a sensing or provenance one. A domain-specific Permit class is needed, the same way manufacturing needed PurchasedKit and retail needed a compliance-rule concept where their reference ontologies didn't reach either.

ASAM OpenODD is the real published standard for describing an Operational Design Domain, but it's an XML/schema-based format, not RDF — this draft borrows its condition-scoping pattern (road class × weather × speed range, each independently validated) for a domain-specific OperationalDesignDomain class rather than claiming a native RDF serialization that doesn't exist.

SoftwareRelease is also domain-specific — PROV-O's Entity and wasRevisionOf cover its version history once it exists, but nothing upstream models "a bundle of independently-versioned components released together under one label."
