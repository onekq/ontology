Insurance

Stage 2 — Draft

Grounded in schema.org's health-insurance vocabulary — real, open, and created for a real regulatory reason: in 2015 the US Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) mandated that health insurers publish machine-readable plan and network data, and schema.org's terms were developed to meet that need.

0. Scope 1. Discover 2. Draft 3. Resolve 4. Validate

Why this domain, not ACORD

ACORD is the real, dominant insurance data-exchange standards body, but its specifications are largely membership-gated XML message schemas, not openly published RDF/OWL. A separate check of insuranceontology.com (an independent, single-vendor project) found it real but thin and "still in development" — not comparable to IOF Core, GoodRelations, FIBO, FHIR, ODRL, or Akoma Ntoso's maturity. schema.org's CMS-driven health-plan vocabulary is the strongest openly-verifiable option for this vertical, at the cost of being narrower — it covers health insurance plan/network/formulary data specifically, not general P&C or life insurance.

Where schema.org doesn't reach

schema.org models the plan/network/formulary data CMS required published, but has no notion of "a SERFF filing status," "a plan variation family," or "a PBM's own separate tier system." Those need domain-specific vocabulary, checked against schema.org first.
