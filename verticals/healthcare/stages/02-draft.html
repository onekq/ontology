Grounded in HL7 FHIR (Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources) — chosen deliberately over SNOMED CT, which is license-restricted in many jurisdictions and would have made the "verify the real spec" research step harder to do openly, the way it worked for IOF Core, GoodRelations, and FIBO.

A structurally different real ontology than the first three

FHIR is event/encounter-centric — a patient's record is a timeline of discrete resources (encounters, conditions, observations), not a static class taxonomy the way IOF Core, GoodRelations, or FIBO mostly are. That shape shows up directly in this vertical's conflicts: several are about a resource's status over time (an encounter that never closed, a condition whose verification status changed after the fact) rather than about which class something belongs to.

Classes adopted from FHIR

ClassDefinition (from the spec)
PatientThe subject of care. Real element link exists specifically for "duplicate or merged patient records," with types replaces/replaced-by/refer/seealso.
EncounterAn interaction between patient and provider — has its own status, separate from any condition's status.
ConditionHas two independent status fields: clinicalStatus (active/inactive/resolved/...) and verificationStatus (unconfirmed/confirmed/refuted/entered-in-error/...). These are real, distinct fields — a downstream system reading only one is a genuine, common integration bug.
AllergyIntoleranceHas its own criticality field (low/high/unable-to-assess), independent of any Condition.

Where FHIR doesn't reach

FHIR models clinical and administrative resources exhaustively, but has no notion of "a family insurance policy's shared member-ID prefix," "a scheduling SLA," or "a care-plan review checklist step." Those need domain-specific vocabulary, checked against FHIR first rather than assumed absent — same discipline as every prior vertical.
