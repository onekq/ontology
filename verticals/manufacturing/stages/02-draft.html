Stage 2 — Draft

Instead of inventing class names from scratch, this draft is built against the Industrial Ontologies Foundry (IOF) Core Ontology — an actively maintained, industry-backed ontology built on BFO. Definitions below are quoted or closely paraphrased from the actual published ontology, not guessed from class names.

0. Scope 1. Discover 2. Draft 3. Resolve 4. Validate

The pattern IOF Core uses: rigid type + role

IOF Core doesn't give a part one fixed class. A physical thing has a rigid type (MaterialArtifact) plus zero or more roles that depend on context and can change. Their own explanatory note on MaterialComponent makes the point directly: "Assemblies that are components for one manufacturer may be final products for another (e.g., diesel engines are Cummins's primary product line, yet a component for its customer Freightliner)." MTR-100 is exactly this — Cairn's product, but a component from an OEM bike partner's perspective. One class hierarchy can't capture that; a role can.

Classes adopted from IOF Core

ClassDefinition (from the ontology)
AssemblyA MaterialArtifact with a component part at all times — permanently composed of parts, not just currently. IOF's own counter-example: "an engine block" doesn't qualify (no separable parts). This maps to Engineering's glossary meaning more precisely than it maps to Procurement's.
MaterialComponentA material entity that bears MaterialComponentRole — a role, not an inherent type.
MaterialProductSame pattern: a material entity bearing MaterialProductRole.
ManufacturerAn Organization bearing ManufacturerRole.
SupplierAn Organization bearing SupplierRole — a distinct role from Manufacturer. One organization can hold both, or only one.
IdentifierDenotes a thing via the denotes/designates relations — the identifier itself is not the thing.
RequirementSpecification / DesignSpecificationDistinct information content entities, linked by requirementSatisfiedBy / satisfiesRequirement.
AssemblyProcessNew in this draft, prompted by the work instruction: a process that produces an Assembly. Not used anywhere in Stages 1-4 of the earlier BOM-only pass — catalog material never needed it.
MeasurementProcess / ProcessCharacteristicNew: a process that measures a quality, and the characteristic being measured. Directly relevant to the Station 2 QC checkpoint — the gap isn't that no measurement process exists, it's that the one specified measures the wrong characteristic (presence, not grade/count).
PlanSpecificationNew: covers the cycle-time/capacity numbers. IOF Core treats a plan as its own information content entity, distinct from the process it plans — useful here since the work instruction and the capacity table are exactly two competing PlanSpecification-shaped documents for the same stations.

Where IOF Core doesn't reach — flagged, not forced

Procurement's "Assembly" meaning — a kitted, purchasable unit — is a commercial concept, not a structural one, and doesn't map cleanly onto IOF Core's structural Assembly class. Rather than force a fit, this stays open going into Resolve: [inferred, contested] — Procurement's meaning likely needs a separate, domain-specific class (e.g. PurchasedKit) related to, not merged with, Assembly.

Similarly, IOF Core has no single first-class "Revision" entity — it models change through temporally-qualified relations (hasComponentPartAtSomeTime vs. hasComponentPartAtAllTimes) rather than a reified version object. Real PLM standards (ISO 10303/STEP AP242) do the opposite — revisions as explicit entities. Both are legitimate; Resolve has to pick one for this ontology rather than silently defaulting to either.
