Conflict 1 of 10
Parent decision — resolve first

Is MTR-100R a variant of MTR-100, or a separate product?

Procurement's service BOM lists it as a child part. Engineering treats it as an independent top-level assembly. It's also sold on the service parts price list as a compatible replacement — see REQ-002 later in this walkthrough for why that claim matters.

Recommended — PROV-O
Model as its own Product, related to MTR-100 by prov:wasDerivedFrom — a real W3C-standard predicate for exactly this "related but independently-tracked" relationship. Not hasPart: MTR-100R isn't a component of MTR-100, it's a separate thing derived from it.
Depends on Conflict 1

ROT-320 uses the same part number for two physically different magnets — and Rev B/Rev C ship concurrently

BOM rows show ROT-320 Rev B (6× N38) and Rev C (8× N42) as if they were the same identifier. Neither has an end date on its effectivity range.

Recommended — IOF Core + PROV-O
Two distinct Identifier instances (ROT-320-RevB, ROT-320-RevC) both denotes-ing physically different parts — the shared display string "ROT-320" is not itself the identifier the ontology should track. Link them with prov:wasRevisionOf (Rev C revises Rev B). For the BOM relationship, use hasComponentPartAtSomeTime rather than AtAllTimes on both — MTR-100R's link points at Rev B, MTR-100 main line's points at Rev C, and neither is globally true.
Conflict 3 of 8

Are the three vendors' FST-004 cross-references the same part?

Meridian is both manufacturer and vendor, exact spec. Tri-Star is vendor-only (manufacturer unrecorded), and its stated length (0.5in) isn't actually 12mm. Value Bolt is a reseller (manufacturer unknown) with a confirmed wrong head type.

Recommended — SKOS + IOF Core + QUDT
Split the ERP's single "Vendor" field into IOF Core's ManufacturerRole and SupplierRole — Tri-Star and Value Bolt hold SupplierRole with ManufacturerRole explicitly unfilled, not silently assumed. For the cross-references: Meridian's gets skos:exactMatch. Tri-Star's gets skos:closeMatch — but only after re-expressing both lengths as proper qudt:QuantityValues (12mm vs. 12.7mm) so the 0.7mm gap is a computed fact, not an eyeballed approximation. Value Bolt's gets no match property at all — a confirmed wrong head type isn't "unverified," it's disproven, and SKOS shouldn't claim otherwise.
Conflict 4 of 8

Is FST-004-2 the same part as FST-004, or different?

Different coating (zinc-nickel vs. zinc), added for outdoor/corrosion-prone mounts. PO shorthand "FST-4-12" has conflated the two for 18 months.

Recommended
Two distinct Part instances. Neither IOF Core nor SKOS has a perfect single-predicate fit here — this isn't a revision (FST-004-2 doesn't replace FST-004 everywhere) and it isn't an equivalence claim (they're deliberately different). Use a domain-specific alternativeTo relation rather than force-fitting a standard one; flag to the user that receiving should log full part numbers, not shorthand.
Conflict 5 of 8

Is "Assembly" one class or two?

Engineering means any BOM node with children. Procurement means a kitted unit purchased as one line item. STA-200 is the former but not the latter.

Recommended — IOF Core, partial fit
Engineering's meaning maps onto IOF Core's own Assembly class directly (a MaterialArtifact with a component part at all times). Procurement's "kitted purchase" meaning is commercial, not structural — IOF Core doesn't reach it. Add a domain-specific PurchasedKit class, related by an optional mayCorrespondTo, rather than forcing it into Assembly.
Conflict 6 of 8

Does "Controller" mean the part or the person?

CTL-400 ("Controller Board Assembly" in ERP) vs. the QA job role "Quality Controller." ECN-124 already renamed the part on engineering drawings for exactly this reason.

Recommended
Name the class MotorControlUnit, matching current drawings. Keep ControllerBoardAssembly as a documented deprecated alias for ERP lookups. No class for the job role — out of scope for a parts ontology, and giving it one would reintroduce the exact collision this conflict is trying to remove.
Conflict 7 of 8

Does "Housing" mean the part or the shipping container?

HSG-500 (cast aluminum motor enclosure) vs. Procurement's outer shipping crate.

Recommended
Two unrelated classes — MotorHousing and ShippingContainer. No shared parent needed; it's purely a vocabulary collision, unlike "Assembly" where a legitimate partial overlap existed.
Conflict 8 of 10

REQ-002 and REQ-014's "Satisfied by" links have gone stale

REQ-002 (45 Nm torque) is only met by Rev C, but MTR-100R (Rev B by default) is marketed as a compatible replacement. REQ-014 (IP65 ingress protection) points at a drawing that predates an undocumented gasket material swap.

Recommended — IOF Core
requirementSatisfiedBy shouldn't be a static, set-once pointer. Model it so that a change to the linked DesignSpecification (or, per Conflict 2, to which Identifier/revision a downstream product actually resolves to) flags the requirement link for re-verification rather than silently continuing to claim satisfaction. This is the conflict where getting the ontology right isn't academic — REQ-002 unresolved means a torque spec is being misrepresented on parts still being sold.
Conflict 9 of 10 — depends on Conflict 2

Station 2's QC checkpoint can't actually catch the ROT-320 Rev B/Rev C difference

The work instruction says "confirm magnets present (visual check)." That passes identically whether the installed rotor is Rev B (6× N38) or Rev C (8× N42) — the one distinction the whole Conflict 2 resolution depends on being tracked correctly downstream of the build line, not just in the BOM.

Recommended — IOF Core
Model Station 2 as an AssemblyProcess with a MeasurementProcess whose hasProcessCharacteristic is explicitly "magnet presence" — not "rotor identifier" or "magnet count." That makes the gap a first-class, queryable fact (this measurement process does not verify which Identifier was installed) instead of something only visible by reading the work instruction's prose closely. The fix on the floor is a different question from the fix in the ontology; the ontology's job here is to make the gap impossible to miss.
Conflict 10 of 10

Two teams maintain two different cycle times for the same stations

The work instruction (Production Engineering) and the capacity table (Production Planning) disagree on Rotor Install (4.5 vs. 3.5 min) and Housing Assembly (5.5 vs. 5.0 min) — same stations, same purpose, maintained by different teams who don't reconcile them.

Recommended — IOF Core
Model the work instruction and the capacity table as two distinct PlanSpecification instances, each with its own hasDateTimeInstantValue-qualified figures, rather than picking one number as "correct" and discarding the other. The disagreement itself is the fact worth keeping — collapsing it into a single number would hide that these two teams are working from different assumptions, which is a bigger problem than either number being wrong.
