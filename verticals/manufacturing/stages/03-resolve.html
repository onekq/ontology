Each conflict from Discover, walked one at a time, in dependency order — a parent classification decision before the children that hang off it. This page shows the conversation as a worked example; when you run this against your own docs, your agent asks these one at a time and waits for your answer instead of dumping them all at once.

Conflict 1 of 7 — parent decision, resolve first

Is MTR-100R a variant of MTR-100, or a separate product?

Procurement's service BOM lists it as a child part. Engineering's drawing register treats it as an independent top-level assembly with its own revision history — and it ships a different rotor spec (6 magnets, Rev B) than current MTR-100 production (8 magnets, Rev C).

Recommended
Model as its own Product, related to MTR-100 by a derivedFrom relation — not hasPart, not a simple variantOf. It diverges in spec and has an independent revision history; collapsing it into MTR-100 as a child would hide that divergence from anyone querying the ontology.
Conflict 2 of 7 — depends on Conflict 1

Does the Rotor Subassembly's Rev B → Rev C count as supersession?

Engineering's glossary assumes Rev C supersedes Rev B outright. ECN-118 says Rev B keeps shipping in field-service kits (now modeled as MTR-100R, per Conflict 1) until ~1,200 units of inventory are depleted.

Recommended
Don't model supersedes as a strict global relation. Instead: Rev C supersedes Rev B scoped to MTR-100 production only — add a shippingContext attribute to each Revision instance so MTR-100R can continue pointing at Rev B without that being treated as stale data.
Conflict 3 of 7

Are the three suppliers' FST-004 cross-references the same part?

Meridian's is an exact spec match. Tri-Star's is 0.7mm longer than spec, marked "equivalent" without engineering sign-off. Value Bolt's is the wrong head type entirely.

Recommended
Three SupplierCrossReference instances, all pointing at the same internal Part (FST-004) — but tag Tri-Star's and Value Bolt's as unverified-equivalent rather than treating all three as certainly identical. That flag is what should surface in a BOM audit; silently accepting them as equal would bury the wrong-head-type problem again.
Conflict 4 of 7

Is FST-004-2 the same part as FST-004, or different?

Different coating (zinc-nickel vs. zinc), added for outdoor/corrosion-prone mounts. PO shorthand "FST-4-12" has conflated the two for 18 months of receiving records.

Recommended
Two distinct Part instances, related by alternativeTo (not supersedes — FST-004-2 doesn't replace FST-004 everywhere, only in corrosion-prone contexts). Separately: flag to the user that receiving should log full part numbers, not shorthand — that's a data-quality fix outside the ontology itself, but worth surfacing since the ontology can't repair already-ambiguous historical records.
Conflict 5 of 7

Is "Assembly" one class or two?

Engineering means any BOM node with children. Procurement means a kitted unit purchased as one line item. STA-200 is the former but not the latter — it's built in-house from individually sourced parts.

Recommended
Keep as two classes — StructuralAssembly (Engineering's meaning) and PurchasedKit (Procurement's) — related by an optional mayCorrespondTo, not merged. They're genuinely different concepts that happen to share an English word, not a naming inconsistency to clean up.
Conflict 6 of 7

Does "Controller" mean the part or the person?

CTL-400 ("Controller Board Assembly" in ERP) vs. the QA job role "Quality Controller." ECN-124 already renamed the part to "Motor Control Unit" on engineering drawings for exactly this reason — ERP was never updated to match.

Recommended
Name the ontology class MotorControlUnit, matching the current engineering drawings. Keep ControllerBoardAssembly as a documented deprecated alias so ERP-based lookups still resolve. Don't create any class for the job role — it's a person attribute, out of scope for a parts ontology, and giving it a class would just reintroduce the collision this conflict is trying to remove.
Conflict 7 of 7

Does "Housing" mean the part or the shipping container?

HSG-500 (cast aluminum motor enclosure) vs. Procurement's outer shipping crate.

Recommended
Two unrelated classes — MotorHousing and ShippingContainer — no shared parent needed. Unlike "Assembly," there's no legitimate overlap case here to model; it's purely a vocabulary collision.

All seven resolved. Next: Stage 4 — Validate.
