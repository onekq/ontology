Manufacturing / Supply Chain

Cairn Drivetrain Co.

A fictional mid-drive motor manufacturer for e-bikes. Its bill of materials, supplier catalog, engineering change log, requirements register, and assembly work instruction all disagree with each other — on purpose. This walkthrough resolves those disagreements into an ontology grounded in real, published vocabulary (IOF Core, PROV-O, SKOS, QUDT), one decision at a time.

Source material

Read these first — the stages below assume you've seen the mess.

Bill of Materials

The MTR-100 motor's part tree — effectivity dates, classification codes, and the MTR-100R/ROT-320 identifier ambiguity.

Supplier Catalog

Three vendors' cross-references for the same fastener, manufacturer identity included.

Engineering Change Log

Rotor revision rationale — and the open question nobody's settled.

Department Glossary

Engineering, Procurement, and QA's separately-maintained term lists.

Requirements Register

Three requirements whose "Satisfied by" links have quietly gone stale.

Assembly Work Instruction

The actual build steps — a QC checkpoint that doesn't check what it needs to, and a cycle-time number two teams don't agree on.

Walk the Build process

Stage 0

Scope

Write the competency questions the ontology needs to answer, before touching any material.

 Stage 1

Discover

Inventory every term and conflict, without resolving anything yet.

 Stage 2

Draft

A first-pass class/relation structure, with inferences marked.

 Stage 3

Resolve

Each conflict, one at a time, with a recommended resolution.

 Stage 4

Validate

Check nothing was silently dropped; produce the changelog.

Result

Final ontology

Class hierarchy + relation set, grounded in real vocabulary.

Turtle serialization

Machine-readable RDF, valid — parses cleanly with real IOF Core / PROV-O / SKOS / QUDT predicates.

Resolution changelog

Every Resolve-stage decision, auditable without re-reading source docs.
