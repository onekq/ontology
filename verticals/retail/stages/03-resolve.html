Conflict 1 of 10
Parent decision — resolve first

When someone says "SKU," do they mean the product or a specific seller's offer?

Alderleaf's catalog uses SKU per product variant. Each marketplace seller submits their own SKU for the same GTIN. schema.org's own definition of sku covers both meanings without distinguishing them — this is a real, documented ambiguity in the standard vocabulary itself, not something specific to Alderleaf.

Recommended — schema.org, made more precise than the standard
Keep sku as a property of Product (Alderleaf's own catalog identifier), and give each Offering its own separate seller_sku property. schema.org doesn't force this distinction, but nothing stops an implementer from making it — and every downstream conflict in this walkthrough depends on not conflating the two.
Depends on Conflict 1

ALD-BP-CASCADE-60-SG still says "60L" after a silent frame redesign

Manufacturer Whitcombe Gear redesigned the frame in 2025 — actual capacity is now 55L, under their own new part number CT55-SG — but issued no new GTIN. Alderleaf's catalog still shows 60L and the old MPN.

Recommended — W3C PROV-O
Model the redesigned product as a distinct Product instance linked by prov:wasRevisionOf to the original — same shared GTIN, but not treated as identical specs. Flag the shared-GTIN-across-a-material-change itself as a data-quality issue: GS1's own guidance says a new GTIN should be issued for a materially different product. That guidance wasn't followed; the ontology should surface that rather than silently trust the GTIN as a stable identity.
Conflict 3 of 10

OutdoorLiquidators' offer contradicts itself

Its item_condition field says "Refurbished." Its own description text says "brand new... never used." Both fields come from the same seller.

Recommended — domain-specific
This isn't the same shape as manufacturing's vendor cross-reference conflict (that was about whether two different sources describe the same real-world part — a genuine SKOS matching problem). Here one seller's own two fields disagree with each other. Assert schema:OfferItemCondition = RefurbishedCondition as the structured field of record, and add a domain-specific descriptionContradictsCondition: true flag rather than silently trusting either field. Forcing this into skos:closeMatch just to reuse the manufacturing pattern would have been the wrong fit.
Conflict 4 of 10

SKU, GTIN, and MPN don't always agree on how many identifiers "the same item" has

Most catalog rows have all three. The Trailmix Cook Pot has no MPN at all — sourced from a marketplace seller who never supplied one.

Recommended — schema.org
Keep sku, gtin13, and mpn as three separate, independent properties (schema.org's actual design — it doesn't require all three, or assume any one implies the others). For the missing MPN: leave it absent and add identifierCompleteness: partial rather than inferring or fabricating a value.
Conflict 5 of 10

Two category trees exist for the same products after a catalog merger

Post-merger codes (e.g. OUT-HIK-PACK-01) exist on catalog rows, but legacy tree names ("Camping > Backpacks") are still referenced by unmigrated BI dashboards.

Recommended — domain-specific
Keep both category trees as separate instances linked by a domain-specific priorCategoryOf relation, rather than deleting the legacy one. The unmigrated dashboards are a real, current consumer of the old tree — collapsing to just the new one would break something that still depends on the old one existing.
Conflict 6 of 10

Does "Listing" mean the storefront page or a seller's offer?

Marketing: one storefront page per product. Marketplace Ops: one per seller per product.

Recommended — schema.org + GoodRelations, no new class needed
Unlike manufacturing's "Assembly" conflict (which needed a new domain-specific class for Procurement's meaning), both meanings here already have a real standard class: Marketing's "listing" is a storefront page backed by schema:AggregateOffer; Marketplace Ops' "listing" is a gr:Offering. No invention required — the ambiguity was just two teams not knowing the other one's term already existed.
Conflict 7 of 10

Two promotions overlap on the same variant

PROMO-114 ($20 off list, through 2026-07-15) and PROMO-121 (flat $99.99, from 2026-07-01) are both valid 2026-07-01 through 2026-07-15 for the Moss Green variant.

Recommended — GoodRelations
Model both as separate gr:UnitPriceSpecification instances with their own validFrom/validThrough — don't merge or pick a winner. The ontology's job is to make the overlap queryable; which promotion actually applies at checkout is a business stacking-order rule, not a fact the ontology itself can resolve. Flagged as a known open question, not silently decided.
Conflict 8 of 10 — highest stakes

The Cascade Headlamp's hazmat requirement is satisfied by the wrong category

REQ-101 requires a hazardous-shipping notice for lithium-battery products. It's satisfied by a flag on Electronics > Batteries — but the headlamp is filed under Outdoor Gear > Lighting, which has no such flag. A real product with a real shipping restriction currently displays no notice.

Recommended — domain-specific
Neither GoodRelations nor schema.org models compliance requirements as a first-class concept, so this needs a domain-specific PolicyRequirement class with a policySatisfiedBy relation — checked against both real vocabularies first; neither fit, so invented deliberately rather than by default (same discipline as manufacturing's alternativeTo). Treat policySatisfiedBy as needing re-verification whenever a product's category changes, not as a static pointer — this is the conflict where the gap has real shipping-compliance consequences, not just modeling tidiness.
Conflict 9 of 10

The returns inspection checkpoint can't catch a substituted item

"Confirm item present, box present" passes identically whether the returned item matches the original order's SKU/GTIN or not.

Recommended — schema.org + domain-specific
Model the return as a real schema:ReturnAction (with object, agent, actionProcess) rather than inventing a bespoke process class — schema.org already covers returns specifically. The gap itself — the inspection step doesn't verify identity — needs a domain-specific verifiesAgainstOriginalOrder: false flag on that step, since neither schema.org nor GoodRelations models "what a process step actually checks" at that granularity. Same shape as manufacturing's QC-checkpoint conflict, different vocabulary because the domain-general parts (the action itself) already have a real, better-fitting standard class here.
Conflict 10 of 10

Marketing and Warehouse Ops don't agree on the fulfillment SLA

Marketing: "ships within 1 business day." Warehouse Ops: average 1.5 business days for non-priority orders. Never reconciled.

Recommended — domain-specific
Model both as separate DeliveryEstimate instances (domain-specific — neither real vocabulary here covers operational planning) rather than reconciled into one number. Same principle as manufacturing's cycle-time conflict: the disagreement between the two teams is the fact worth keeping, not something to average away.
