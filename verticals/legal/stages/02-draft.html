FRBR, reused correctly this time

Akoma Ntoso's FRBRWork/FRBRExpression/FRBRManifestation layering is the same FRBR model referenced loosely elsewhere in library science, but here it's a real, precise part of a ratified OASIS standard: Work is the abstract ordinance, Expression is one dated/amended version of its text, Manifestation is a specific rendering (the posted PDF, the canonical XML). This directly resolves the "Ordinance 2019-14" ambiguity from Discover.

Classes adopted from Akoma Ntoso

ClassDefinition (from the spec)
FRBRWorkThe abstract ordinance as a legal concept, independent of any specific text.
FRBRExpressionOne specific, dated version of the ordinance's text — what changes when an amendment takes effect.
FRBRManifestationA specific rendering of an Expression — a PDF, an XML file. Can drift out of sync with its Expression, as TMC 14.20.030's posted PDF did.
hierarchical elements (section, article, paragraph, ...)Real, named structural elements — not a generic "part" concept invented for this vertical.

Relations and attributes adopted from Akoma Ntoso

Relation/attributeDefinition
activeModifications / passiveModificationsWhat one provision does to another, and what's been done to a given provision, respectively. Real, and specifically two-sided — a gap in one doesn't mean a gap in the underlying law, just in the record.
@start / @endValidity period attributes. Per the spec: "an @end attribute with no @start attribute mark a fragment that was part of the original document but has been repealed" — a precise, real rule, not an invented convention.
lifecycle / temporalDataThe container for every event affecting a document, with dates and references to the generating instrument.
ref (@href)Cross-references between provisions.

Where Akoma Ntoso doesn't reach

Akoma Ntoso models legal document structure exhaustively but has no notion of "a records request SLA," "a filing-folder section," or "which office uses which vocabulary." Those need domain-specific terms, checked against the spec first.
