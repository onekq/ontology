Government

Stage 2 — Draft

Grounded in two real, open standards: the W3C Organization Ontology (ORG) for organizational structure, and the EU's Core Public Service Vocabulary Application Profile (CPSV-AP) for public service description.

0. Scope 1. Discover 2. Draft 3. Resolve 4. Validate

Two standards, deliberately combined

Neither vocabulary covers the whole domain alone. ORG models who the organization is (departments, hierarchy, reporting lines); CPSV-AP models what it does (services, rules, requirements, cost). A government ontology needs both — the same "combine two real, narrower ontologies rather than force-fit one" move already made once for insurance (schema.org) but here needed twice, deliberately, from the start.

Classes adopted

ClassSourceDefinition
OrganizationalUnitW3C ORG"An Organization such as a department or support unit which is part of some larger Organization." Resolves HR's meaning of "Department" directly.
PublicServiceCPSV-AP"A mandatory or discretionary set of activities performed... by or on behalf of a public organisation."
RuleCPSV-APThe document setting out a service's specific rules/procedures — including fee schedules.
Requirement / EvidenceCPSV-APDeliberately distinct: "Requirement... condition or prerequisite... to be proven by Evidence." The spec itself names exactly the Discover-stage conflation.
CostCPSV-AP"Any costs related to the execution of a Public Service that the Agent consuming it needs to pay."
LifeEvent / BusinessEventCPSV-APTwo distinct real trigger-event classes — already separate concepts, which is why Conflict 5's ambiguity is a documentation gap, not a modeling one.

Relations adopted

RelationSourceDefinition
hasCompetentAuthorityCPSV-APLinks a Public Service to the responsible Public Organization — the exact relation Conflict 3's reorg broke.
holdsRequirementCPSV-APLinks a service to its Requirements — real property name, not "hasRequirement" as might be guessed.
hasSubOrganization / subOrganizationOfW3C ORGThe real hierarchical relation the 2024 reorg actually changed.

Where neither standard reaches

Neither ORG nor CPSV-AP models "a website navigation category," "a channel-specific fee-waiver field," or "a fee-schedule reconciliation process." Those need domain-specific vocabulary, checked against both standards first.
